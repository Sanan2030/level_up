## Concept

The uploaded document is specifically The Kanban Guide for Scrum Teams, January 2021. It complements Scrum and introduces no additional required events. Its four practices are visualizing workflow, limiting WIP, actively managing work in progress, and inspecting and adapting the Definition of Workflow. Make start/finish points, work items, states and movement policies explicit.

## Why it matters

WIP counts started but unfinished items. Cycle Time is elapsed time from start to finish. Work Item Age is elapsed time from start until now for unfinished work. Throughput counts items finished per unit of time, not summed story points. WIP is an observation; a WIP limit is a policy. Pull means starting work when capacity becomes available.

## Decision principles

An SLE combines elapsed time and probability, for example 85% of items finish within eight days. It is a forecast based on historical Cycle Time, not a guarantee for every item. Without history, start with a guess and adapt. Little’s Law relates average Cycle Time to average WIP divided by average Throughput; consistent boundaries and stable long-run flow assumptions matter. It is not an individual-item deadline calculator.

## Worked scenario

The WIP limit is four and four items are open. One is nine days old; SLE is eight days at 85%. Another team asks you to start more work.

## Decision reasoning

Inspect the aging and blocked item and help finish work before starting more. Do not automatically raise the WIP limit. At Daily, discuss the flow risk to the Sprint Goal and an unblocking action. Inspect policies and the limit’s effect in Retrospective, adapting earlier when needed.

## Common pitfalls

Applying age to finished work; treating SLE as a guarantee; raising WIP limits whenever work stalls; abolishing Sprints because Kanban is used.

## Application exercise

An item starts on day 2 and finishes on day 7. Another starts on day 3 and is unfinished on day 9. Ten items finished last week. Identify the metrics.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

Using elapsed-time differences, the first item’s Cycle Time is five days and the second’s Age six days. Throughput is ten items per week. Total WIP cannot be calculated without all open items. Use consistent start/finish timestamps and day conventions.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

WIP: open-item count; Cycle Time: finished-item duration; Age: unfinished-item age; Throughput: finished items/time; SLE: time + probability.
