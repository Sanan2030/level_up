## Concept

Scrum uses empiricism to make decisions from observed experience in uncertain work. Transparency makes the meaning of work and results visible, inspection compares that information with progress toward goals, and adaptation turns learning into a changed decision. Meetings and dashboards alone do not create this chain. If a team hides unfavorable results, even frequent events provide an unreliable basis for decisions.

## Why it matters

The Scrum Guide identifies Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect and Courage. Commitment concerns goals and mutual support, not preserving every initial estimate unchanged. Focus concerns Sprint work and progress toward goals. Openness supports revealing challenges; Respect treats colleagues as capable, independent people; Courage supports raising difficult issues and doing the right thing. These are social conditions for transparency, not merely five words to memorize.

## Decision principles

A self-managing Scrum Team internally decides who does what, when and how. This does not remove shared goals or quality boundaries. Product Owner accountability for value and backlog management, Developers’ planning and quality accountabilities, and the Scrum Master’s effectiveness accountability remain. The book’s Vision section explains how direction and boundaries support autonomous decisions. Share the problem, goal and constraints instead of prescribing every technical action, and allow the team to propose a better approach.

## Worked scenario

A booking team hides an unfinished integration check while reporting 95% completion. Developers want to reveal the uncertainty, but a manager has threatened bonuses for bad news. The Product Owner forecasts release from that dashboard.

## Decision reasoning

First expose the actual state: what is Done, which checks remain, and which outcome is still unknown? The purpose is a sound forecast rather than blame. The Product Owner communicates uncertainty, Developers create a safe completion plan, and the Scrum Master works with the organization on the punishment barrier. Penalizing honest risk reporting encourages distortion in the next report too.

## Common pitfalls

Confusing transparency with surveillance of every person; equating Commitment with an immutable scope promise; using Courage to excuse disrespect; interpreting self-management as working without Product Goal or Done. Naming a value without explaining the behavior that supports or undermines it is weak reasoning.

## Application exercise

Write a two-sentence update: first state a known fact, then a risk and adaptation. Explain which two Scrum values should be visible. Teaching assumption: the security check is incomplete, while some working functionality is already integrated.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

“Some functionality is integrated, but the agreed security check is incomplete. We are updating the Done forecast with that uncertainty and adapting the completion plan with Developers.” Openness appears in sharing facts and Courage in raising unwelcome news; Respect keeps the discussion constructive. Do not invent a precise release date where the evidence is missing.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Transparency → meaningful inspection → timely adaptation. Values are behaviors that make the chain trustworthy. Self-management works together with clear goals and quality boundaries.
