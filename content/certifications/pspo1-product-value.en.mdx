## Concept

Chapters 1–4 of The Professional Product Owner explain product management through Vision, Value and Validation. Vision establishes purpose and audience, Value identifies a beneficial change, and Validation tests that assumption against real use. Finishing a project on time does not by itself prove value. A product mindset considers outcomes throughout the product’s life.

## Why it matters

EBM 2024 distinguishes inputs, activities, outputs, customer outcomes and organizational impacts. Budget is an input, interviews an activity, a released feature an output, easier task completion an outcome, and sustainable revenue growth an impact. These are not interchangeable. Counting features without checking user benefit can conceal poor investments.

## Decision principles

Chapter 7 considers value, risk, cost/size and dependencies in backlog ordering. Scrum mandates no ranking formula. A small learning step can reduce the risk of a large investment. Developers size the work; the Product Owner compares evidence and makes ordering transparent. User stories, story points, MVPs and roadmaps can be useful practices, but are not mandatory Scrum artifacts.

## Worked scenario

The team ships 12 features but booking completion remains 40%. Sales requests a new report; observations suggest users do not understand slot selection.

## Decision reasoning

Keep 40% as the baseline. Test a small simplification to slot selection and compare completion for comparable users. Feature count is output; more people successfully booking is an outcome. Compare the report’s expected impact separately.

## Common pitfalls

Always ordering by stakeholder rank; treating velocity as value; applying a ranking formula without judgment.

## Application exercise

Compare a 20-day report with a three-day slot-selection experiment. What evidence would change the choice?

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

Compare customer impact, credible risks, estimated size, dependencies and learning value. The experiment may reduce uncertainty, while a legal deadline or critical dependency could change ordering. All numbers are teaching assumptions.

## Check understanding and remember

Vision → direction; Value → expected benefit; Validation → user evidence. Output ≠ outcome. Ordering considers value, risk, size and dependency.
