## Concept

A product idea is an assumption about an outcome. EBM 2024 makes that assumption explicit and tests progress through small steps toward a goal. Discovery is used here as a teaching term for learning about users, problems and potential value; Scrum does not require a separate Discovery Sprint or Discovery Team. Research and experimentation are part of the Scrum Team’s product-related work.

## Why it matters

Chapter 4 of the book distinguishes stakeholder feedback from market validation. Sponsor approval at Review is information, but does not prove that users can achieve a better outcome. An MVP approach selects a small step sufficient to test a critical technical or market assumption; it need not contain every planned feature. Prototypes and interviews can examine some assumptions. State what they cannot establish as well.

## Decision principles

Before testing, state the audience, baseline, expected change, observation window and decision rule. EBM supports measurable outcomes and adaptation; the comparison-group, sampling-bias and harm-guardrail questions here are editorial experiment-design advice. Weak evidence does not justify a precise causal claim. Raising one local metric while damaging user trust may fail to create sustainable value.

## Worked scenario

In a learning product, half of new users never start a first practice session. A sponsor requests more reminders. The team plans a large notification system, while some users report not understanding how to choose a topic.

## Decision reasoning

Observe the problem and separate competing explanations: users cannot find a topic, do not understand practice value, or genuinely need reminders. A smaller topic-selection screen and a limited reminder are different test options. Changing everything at once makes learning harder. Inspect practice completion and complaints as well as starts after testing.

## Common pitfalls

Pitfalls include treating the requested feature as the underlying problem, presenting internal applause as market benefit, generalizing from a few enthusiastic users, ignoring harm because clicks increased, and continuing a disproved assumption because of past spending.

## Application exercise

Write a one-sentence hypothesis: for whom will which change produce what outcome? Add an observation that could refute it and a condition limiting possible user harm.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

One hypothesis: simpler topic selection helps new users finish their first practice. Compare comparable users with the baseline and verify measurement quality. If completion does not improve and confusion complaints rise, do not expand the solution. The purpose is to make a better decision about the need, not to defend a favored implementation.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Problem → explicit hypothesis → small test → outcome and harm → adaptation. Output, customer outcome and organizational impact are distinct observations.
