## Learning objectives

- Separate pricing, packaging and revenue assumptions.
- Calculate contribution and a simplified break-even point.
- Design a launch experiment with adoption and harm measures.

## Why this matters

A product can attract registrations without supporting a sustainable business. Pricing and go-to-market decisions connect customer value, alternatives, acquisition, delivery cost and retention. A launch is a test of those assumptions, not proof that demand exists because marketing activity was completed.

## Core theory

Pricing sets what is charged and under which terms. Packaging groups capabilities, limits and service levels into offers. A revenue model describes how money is earned over time. A low advertised price may still create a poor experience if unavoidable fees or restrictive terms are unclear.

Understand the customer situation, alternatives and purchasing authority. Interview actual decision-makers and examine current spending and trade-offs. Hypothetical willingness to pay is evidence to investigate, not a guaranteed purchase. An experiment can test an offer, but it must represent availability and conditions honestly.

Unit economics connects revenue and variable costs at a defined unit such as customer-month or completed transaction. Contribution per unit is price minus the included variable cost. State which costs are included; support, payment processing, infrastructure and refunds can change the result. Contribution is not the same as net profit.

In a simplified single-product model, break-even volume equals fixed costs divided by contribution per unit, provided the contribution is positive and assumptions hold. Real businesses may have capacity steps, multiple plans, discounts, taxes and changing retention. Avoid using a neat formula to conceal uncertain inputs.

A go-to-market hypothesis identifies the target segment, value proposition, channel, purchasing process, onboarding and support needed for adoption. Define qualified reach, trial, paid conversion, retained usage and relevant guardrails. A campaign can generate cheap leads that never become suitable customers. Review channel cost and retained value together, and avoid comparing cohorts with incomplete follow-up.

## Worked example

An original subscription example charges 50 currency units per customer-month and includes 20 units of variable cost per customer-month. Contribution is `50 - 20 = 30`. With 60000 units of monthly fixed costs, simplified break-even is `60000 / 30 = 2000` active paying customers.

If variable cost rises to 35, contribution becomes 15 and the simplified break-even doubles to 4000 customers. A large signup count does not repair weak contribution or poor retention. The team tests a small offer with transparent terms and measures paid activation, a complete retention window and support burden before scaling the channel.

## Inspect the decision

<details>
<summary>A cheaper acquisition channel produces more signups. Should its budget automatically increase?</summary>

No. Check whether the signups are eligible customers, activate, pay and remain long enough to support the economics. Compare complete cohorts and included costs. Cheap signups can be expensive if support burden, refunds or early churn are high.

</details>

## Common mistakes

- Equating stated interest with purchase behavior.
- Calling contribution net profit.
- Ignoring discounts, refunds or delivery costs.
- Using an incomplete recent cohort to claim superior retention.

## Practice exercise

At a price of 40 and variable cost of 25 per customer-month, calculate contribution and simplified break-even for fixed costs of 45000. Name two assumptions that could invalidate the forecast and two launch measures.

## Worked solution

<details>
<summary>Show worked solution</summary>

Contribution is 15 per customer-month and simplified break-even is 3000 active paying customers. Changing variable cost, plan mix, capacity or retention can alter the calculation. Measure a defined paid-activation outcome and retained usage over a complete window, while monitoring support or refund harm. Record acquisition costs separately and define the time horizon before comparing customer value with acquisition spending.

</details>

## Check understanding and cheat sheet

Situation → honest offer → price and cost assumptions → channel experiment → complete adoption/retention evidence. Contribution and signups are not net profit or retained demand.
