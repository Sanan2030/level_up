## Concept

Complex product work does not allow every cause and effect to be known in advance. Scrum uses empiricism and lean thinking to inspect evidence and adapt. The book’s Empiricism chapter offers models for understanding complexity; these are not additional Scrum rules. Small testable steps help limit exposure to a large mistaken investment.

## Why it matters

Chapter 8 recommends using historical Done work and communicating uncertainty when forecasting. Forecasting informs delivery expectations; it does not prove value. Update forecasts when dependencies or work characteristics change. A fixed date can prompt scope discussion, but does not justify lowering quality or counting unfinished work as Done.

## Decision principles

When multiple Scrum Teams work on one product, they share the Product Goal, Product Backlog and Product Owner. They need a shared Definition of Done for a working integrated Increment. Increasing each team’s local output may not improve end-to-end product flow. EBM’s T2M and A2I can expose these constraints.

## Worked scenario

Three teams call their components finished, but integration takes two weeks. A customer requests a release date.

## Decision reasoning

Do not claim the product is Done before integration. Expose shared quality boundaries and the integration constraint. Forecast from comparable integrated work, explain uncertainty and assumptions, and investigate a smaller valuable scope.

## Common pitfalls

Equating component output with product value; weakening Done to meet a date; assuming more people automatically increase speed.

## Application exercise

Write a three-sentence forecast for the customer: evidence, uncertainty and next update.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

Past integrated work can establish a forecast range. A new dependency may change that range. Update after the next Done Increment. No unsupported precise date is invented.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Integrated evidence; shared Done; explicit uncertainty; updated forecasts.
