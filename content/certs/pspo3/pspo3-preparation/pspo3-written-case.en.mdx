## Concept

This is a written product-reasoning exercise, not an official grading rubric. A strong answer separates facts, assumptions and missing information. In EBM 2024, shipping a feature is not a customer outcome. Establish the audience and valuable outcome first; a merchant completing reconciliation may be more informative than feature count.

## Why it matters

Compare at least two plausible options and their costs. Chapter 7’s value, risk, size and dependencies help structure the comparison. Make a concrete recommendation: what now, what later, and why? The Product Owner retains ordering accountability, while Developers own sizing and technical planning. Describing collaboration need not avoid a decision.

## Decision principles

End with an inspection plan: baseline, expected outcome, observation window, guardrail and evidence that would change the decision. Do not invent missing numbers; explain how to gather them. This structure is our teaching aid. Length and terminology alone do not establish sound judgment.

## Worked scenario

A payments product ships all planned features while merchant retention falls. The team proposes a larger dashboard.

## Decision reasoning

Do not immediately expand the dashboard. Investigate needs with departing merchants, form a testable hypothesis such as reconciliation friction, and compare the impact of a smaller solution. Track retention alongside operational errors and update the decision from outcomes.

## Common pitfalls

Inventing facts; omitting alternatives; saying “I would discuss it” without a decision or inspection plan.

## Application exercise

Respond in 150–250 words. Mark the fact, options, decision and inspection sentences. This length is a practice suggestion only.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

One answer: retention decline is a fact; the cause remains an assumption. Compare a full dashboard with a smaller reconciliation improvement. First choose limited research to validate the problem. Then inspect retention and errors for comparable users; change the approach if evidence fails to support it.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Fact → alternatives → reasoned decision → inspection. Make unknowns explicit.
