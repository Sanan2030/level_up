## Concept

EBM’s experiment loop forms a hypothesis, runs an experiment, inspects results and adapts. A disproved assumption should inform the next decision. The book’s Validation chapter discusses pivoting and persevering. Continuing needs an evidence-based rationale; prior investment alone is insufficient.

## Why it matters

Separate learning about the product from learning about how work is done. Review inspects product outcomes and future adaptations with stakeholders. Retrospective plans improvements to quality and effectiveness. New evidence need not be hidden until these events. Reinspect the goal if it no longer fits; doing more of the same may not help.

## Decision principles

For self-review ask: what did I assume, which alternative did I miss, who could be harmed, and what observation would change my mind? This is our teaching rubric, not the official PSPO assessment system. Clearer reasoning neither justifies inventing facts nor means every option is equally defensible.

## Worked scenario

A new notification increases clicks. The team declares success, but complaints increase and booking completion stays unchanged.

## Decision reasoning

Acknowledge that clicks substituted for the intended outcome. Inspect completion and complaint guardrails. Consider stopping or changing the notification if harm rises. Define outcome criteria before the next experiment.

## Common pitfalls

Selecting only favorable metrics; redefining success after results; framing learning only as personal blame.
