## 1. Learning objectives

Explain the distinct accountabilities of Product Owner, Scrum Master and Developers; distinguish delegation from accountability; and reason through a realistic interruption without inventing a project-manager role inside Scrum.

## 2. Why it matters

A team building a bank’s card-freeze feature receives an urgent request directly from a senior sales manager. One developer is told to drop the current Sprint work. The Product Owner is unaware, and the Scrum Master is asked to approve the code design. Confused accountability produces hidden work and weakens the team’s ability to inspect progress.

## 3. Core theory

In the Scrum Guide 2020, one Scrum Team contains a Product Owner, a Scrum Master and Developers. These are accountabilities, not a required mapping to employment titles. The team is cross-functional and self-managing.

**Product Owner:** accountable for maximizing the product’s value and for effective Product Backlog management. This includes communicating the Product Goal, making backlog items understandable, ordering the backlog and ensuring transparency. Work can be delegated; accountability is retained. The Product Owner is one person, not a committee.

**Developers:** accountable for creating a usable Increment each Sprint, creating the Sprint Backlog plan, maintaining quality through the Definition of Done, adapting their daily plan toward the Sprint Goal and holding each other accountable as professionals. “Developers” includes people with the skills needed to create the Increment, not only programmers.

**Scrum Master:** accountable for establishing Scrum as described in the guide and for the Scrum Team’s effectiveness. The Scrum Master coaches self-management, helps remove impediments and supports the team, Product Owner and organization in using Scrum effectively. This is not a default assignment authority or a code-approval gate.

Collaboration is essential. Distinct accountability does not mean working in silos. The whole team shares product context and inspects outcomes.

## 4. Worked example: an urgent card-freeze request

The developer makes the request visible rather than accepting secret work. The Product Owner clarifies the customer impact and urgency with the requester. Developers explain the current Sprint Goal, available options and technical implications. The team collaborates on any necessary scope adaptation without endangering the Sprint Goal.

If the Sprint Goal has become obsolete, only the Product Owner has authority to cancel the Sprint. Cancellation is not a routine shortcut for every urgent request. The Scrum Master helps the organization understand the accountabilities and prevents repeated hidden assignment from becoming normal.

| Decision                                          | Primary accountability                           | Useful collaboration                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product Backlog ordering                          | Product Owner                                    | Users, stakeholders and Developers supply evidence       |
| How selected work is turned into an Increment     | Developers                                       | Product Owner clarifies value and trade-offs             |
| Coaching the organization in Scrum                | Scrum Master                                     | Leaders and the whole team participate                   |
| Inspecting product progress and future adaptation | Scrum Team and stakeholders in the Sprint Review | Product Owner discusses progress toward the Product Goal |

## 6. Interactive explainer

Walk through the cycle and name which accountability contributes at each point. More than one usually contributes; ask what each is accountable for rather than who is allowed to attend.

<summary>Can the Product Owner ask someone else to refine a backlog item?</summary>

Yes. Work may be delegated, and refinement is collaborative. The Product Owner remains accountable for effective Product Backlog management. Delegating a task does not distribute the Product Owner accountability across a committee.

## 7. Common mistakes and best practices

- **Mistake:** “Developers” means programmers only. **Improve:** include the skills required to create a usable Increment.
- **Mistake:** the Scrum Master assigns everyone’s daily tasks. **Improve:** coach self-management and help address impediments.
- **Mistake:** the Product Owner must write every backlog item alone. **Improve:** collaborate while keeping accountability clear.
- **Mistake:** an influential stakeholder can silently replace Sprint work. **Improve:** make the need visible and discuss adaptation and the Sprint Goal.
- **Mistake:** quality can be suspended to meet a date. **Improve:** inspect scope and options while respecting the Definition of Done.

## 8. Practice exercise and sample solution

**Exercise:** A compliance specialist identifies a risk in a payment feature during a Sprint. Describe what the Product Owner, Developers and Scrum Master could do. Avoid assigning all decisions to one person.

<details>
<summary>Reveal a sample solution</summary>

The Product Owner clarifies impact, customer value and urgency with compliance and makes any backlog decision transparent. Developers inspect the technical implications and adapt the plan with the Product Owner while considering the Sprint Goal and Definition of Done. The Scrum Master helps the team and stakeholders collaborate effectively and addresses organizational obstacles. If the Sprint Goal is obsolete, the Product Owner can cancel the Sprint. The correct response depends on evidence; “ignore compliance until the next Sprint” and “automatically restart every Sprint” are both poor blanket rules.

</details>

## 9. Mini-quiz

**1. Who is accountable for effective Product Backlog management?**

A. The Product Owner. B. A stakeholder committee. C. The Scrum Master by default.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**A.** Others can contribute, but the Product Owner retains accountability. B replaces the single accountable person with a committee. C confuses coaching Scrum with owning product ordering.

</details>

**2. Which statement about Developers is correct?**

A. They only implement specifications and never adapt their plan. B. They create a usable Increment and adapt their plan toward the Sprint Goal. C. They are exclusively programmers.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**B.** Developers own the plan and quality of creating the Increment. A removes self-management. C narrows the accountability to a job title.

</details>

**3. A Scrum Master’s default job is to assign daily tasks. True or false?**

A. True. B. False.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**B, false.** The Scrum Master supports effectiveness and self-management. A introduces a command-and-control assignment role that the Scrum Guide does not prescribe.

</details>

**4. What happens when the Product Owner delegates work on a backlog item?**

A. Accountability automatically transfers to the person doing the work. B. Product Owner accountability remains with the Product Owner. C. The Product Owner becomes a committee.

<details>
<summary>Answer and explanation</summary>

**B.** Delegation and accountability are different. A confuses doing a task with retaining accountability. C contradicts the single Product Owner accountability.

</details>

## 10. One-page cheat sheet

**Product Owner:** product value, Product Goal and effective Product Backlog management. **Developers:** usable Increment, Sprint Backlog plan, quality and daily adaptation. **Scrum Master:** Scrum as defined and team effectiveness. **Whole Scrum Team:** cross-functional, self-managing collaboration. **Interruption response:** make the need visible, clarify urgency, inspect options and protect coherent decision-making. **Study habit:** explain a scenario in your own words and link your reasoning to the official guide.
