## Concept

The Sprint is a fixed-length container of one month or less. The next Sprint begins immediately. In Planning the Scrum Team addresses why the Sprint is valuable, what can be done and how. The Sprint Goal is finalized before Planning ends; Developers select the work. Planning is at most eight hours for a one-month Sprint.

## Why it matters

The Daily Scrum is 15 minutes every working day for Developers to inspect progress toward the Sprint Goal and adapt their plan. A Product Owner or Scrum Master actively working on Sprint Backlog items participates as a Developer. It is not a status report to the Product Owner. Plans may also change outside the event.

## Decision principles

Sprint Review is a working session with stakeholders to inspect outcomes, progress toward the Product Goal and environmental changes. Retrospective plans improvements to quality and effectiveness. For a one-month Sprint, Review is at most four hours and Retrospective three; usually shorter for shorter Sprints. Review is not a release approval gate: a Done Increment can be delivered earlier.

### Identify the event by its purpose

An issue can inform more than one event. A delayed release may affect future product choices in Review and prompt integration-practice improvements in Retrospective. Explain the event through the decision it supports. The Scrum Guide does not prescribe three Daily questions, a slide format for Review, or a particular Retrospective facilitation game. Important new product information need not wait until the next event to be shared.

## Worked scenario

On day six of a two-week Sprint a useful Increment is Done. The sponsor wants to wait until Review to release and use Review only for slides.

## Decision reasoning

Scrum does not require waiting for Review. Delivery can occur earlier, considering operational and risk conditions. Use Review to inspect the usable outcome and user evidence together and adapt next steps.

## Common pitfalls

Using Daily as management reporting; making Review an approval gate; blaming people in Retrospective; inventing a required Scrum event for every concern.

## Application exercise

Place three topics: changed customer needs, blocked work, and a recurring testing problem.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

Customer and product adaptation fits Review; blocked progress toward the Sprint Goal fits Daily; improving testing practices fits Retrospective. Important issues need not wait for an event.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Sprint ≤ one month; Planning ≤ eight hours; Daily 15 minutes; Review ≤ four hours; Retrospective ≤ three hours. The hourly limits apply to a one-month Sprint; Daily remains 15 minutes.
