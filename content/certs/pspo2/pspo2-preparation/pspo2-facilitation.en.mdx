## Concept

The Product Owner is one person, while sound product decisions draw on many people’s knowledge. The Scrum Guide says stakeholders may influence the Product Backlog by convincing the Product Owner. This neither requires majority voting nor makes one person a communication bottleneck. Developers can collaborate directly with users and domain experts.

## Why it matters

The book’s Validation chapter distinguishes early stakeholder feedback from market validation. In Review, inspect outcomes together rather than collecting approval. When sales, support and users disagree, separate the problem, affected audience and expected outcome. Listening to everyone does not mean implementing every request simultaneously.

## Decision principles

A practical facilitation technique compares options consistently: who is affected, what changes, what evidence exists, what is at risk, and when will we revisit? Developers contribute feasibility and sizing; the Product Owner explains ordering and trade-offs. The Scrum Master can support stakeholder collaboration when needed. These questions are teaching techniques, not mandatory Scrum procedure.

### Choose the appropriate support stance

The supplied Scrum.org framework names facilitation, leadership styles, coaching, mentoring and teaching. A useful teaching distinction is that facilitation supports group thinking, teaching explains a missing concept, mentoring offers options informed by experience, and coaching helps people examine their own reasoning. These are practical explanations around the listed stances, not additional Scrum accountabilities. Whichever stance is useful, preserve Product Owner accountability and Developers’ ownership of their plan.

## Worked scenario

Sales wants a custom export for a large customer; support wants to fix lost bookings affecting many users. Both say their request is urgent.

## Decision reasoning

Compare lost bookings, contractual exposure, size and smaller options. Stakeholder rank is not the sole criterion. The Product Owner states the decision, its cost and what new evidence would change it.

## Common pitfalls

Starting everything halfway as a compromise; requiring unanimity; replacing the user problem with an authority dispute.

## Application exercise

Resolve the dispute in five sentences: goal, evidence, two options, decision, revisit condition.

## Explained solution

<details>
<summary>Show explained solution</summary>

The goal is reliable booking. Loss affects many users. Full and smaller export options exist. Fix booking loss first, then deliver a smaller export. Reinspect if a verified contractual deadline changes exposure. This is one defensible answer, not a universal ordering rule.

</details>

## Check understanding and remember

Listen → compare evidence → explain the decision → name revisit conditions. Collaboration does not erase accountability.
